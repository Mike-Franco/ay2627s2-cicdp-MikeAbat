## Owner-approved first receipt rules — 5 November 2026

For an authenticated active Manager or Staff member, accept manual receipt only for an inventory in the same business with a current active assignment and a product active and linked to that inventory. Recheck and lock authorization in the write transaction. Assignment revocation committed first denies the receipt; if the receipt obtains its required authorization locks first, it may complete before revocation. Accept positive whole-number quantities in the product's labeled base unit; packaging conversion is deferred. Require expiry configuration before a product's first receipt. When mandatory expiry tracking is enabled, a valid expiry date is required. When it is not enabled, a manual receipt may omit expiry; if supplied, reject a date that is today or earlier in Asia/Singapore and apply expiry eligibility and dated FEFO. An undated receipt remains in the approved undated ordering. Existing products remain active. Implement product lifecycle and configuration fields `is_active` and `expiry_tracking_required`; no ordinary receipt is allowed for an inactive product.

Each receipt is at most 2,147,483,647 units, and the product/inventory physical balance may not exceed 2,147,483,647 units, counting quarantined stock. Reject any overflow atomically. A receiving form has a unique request token: same token plus identical receipt input returns the original receipt; same token with changed input is rejected; a fresh form/token permits a separate receipt. Recheck authorization even when returning the original result. Create one stable batch identity, committed receiving sequence, and exactly one attributable receipt audit event in the same PostgreSQL transaction. Any validation, authorization, batch, sequence, balance or audit failure leaves no receipt mutation committed.

These are owner-approved observable requirements, not claims of implementation, full SRS approval or Design entry. `Product.is_active`, `Product.expiry_tracking_required`, a base-unit label, batch identity/sequence fields, balance and audit records do not yet exist in TORY-13. The approved names `is_active` and `expiry_tracking_required` must be implemented later. How to store the label, request token/input fingerprint, balances and audit detail remains Design work. InventoryProduct existence represents a link; it has no independent active flag.

## Proposed PostgreSQL transaction and locking protocol — NOT APPROVED

Use the business-owned **Product row as the shared anchor** for all receipt operations for that product and any future cross-inventory deactivation. A proposed sequence is:

1. Authenticate, enforce CSRF, validate basic form syntax and perform an initial authorization check before taking locks. Do not reveal product/inventory details to an unauthorized caller.
2. Start `transaction.atomic()` and lock the Product row `FOR UPDATE`. This serializes compliant receipt writers for the same product across inventories and gives future deactivation one row to lock before checking every business inventory.
3. After the Product lock, recheck the active account/membership, same-business inventory, and active assignment. Lock the membership and applicable assignment row through commit in a consistent order. The approved outcome is that revocation committed first denies; a receipt holding the required authorization locks first may complete before revocation. The exact lock mode and stale-account behavior still require implementation review.
4. Lock/confirm the Inventory and InventoryProduct association in a globally consistent order. The current TORY-13 association trigger locks Inventory then Product on link insert/update. That order can deadlock with a Product-first receipt if a link update holds the association/inventory path while waiting on Product. Before using Product as the anchor, TORY-16 Design must reconcile the existing trigger and all association mutations to one Product-before-Inventory order, or prove another protocol that avoids this cycle. Do not claim the current trigger order is already compatible.
5. Read one authoritative server time after lock acquisition, convert it to Asia/Singapore, and reject expiry dates on or before that local date. Asia/Singapore date semantics are approved; choosing PostgreSQL or another trusted clock source and handling clock failure remain technical review items.
6. While Product remains locked, allocate the next sequence for that product/inventory and create the receiving batch plus exactly one receipt event. A proposed simple allocation is `MAX(sequence)+1` under the Product lock, with a uniqueness constraint on product, inventory and sequence. All manual/CSV receipt writers must use the same lock. The lock serializes allocation for the product; it does not itself guarantee correct authorization, audit fields, batch validity or stockout allocation.
7. Commit batch and receipt event together. On any exception, roll back the entire transaction. PostgreSQL lock waits serialize same-product work. The approved request token must make same-input retries return the committed original and changed-input reuse fail; authorization is rechecked before either outcome. How to fingerprint normalized input, persist tokens, handle concurrent token requests, and respond after timeout/deadlock remains for Design. Any retry starts a fresh transaction and reauthorizes. No timeout or retry count is approved.

The Product lock protects only operations that acquire it and hold it through commit. Future ordinary stock-out and every deactivation path must use the same anchor if they can change/check physical stock. Deactivation must lock Product before checking all inventories; then either receipt commits first and the zero-stock check fails, or deactivation commits first and a later receipt sees inactive state and fails. The Product lock does not alone protect assignment freshness, InventoryProduct link removal, stock arithmetic, noncompliant direct SQL, or audit atomicity. Those need their own row locks/constraints and transaction tests. Sequence numbers allocated inside the transaction roll back with failed receipts; committed sequence order is the receiving order, not a promise of gapless IDs if a database sequence is chosen instead.

## Open decisions and bounded deferrals

### Remaining before TORY-16 implementation/acceptance

- Implement the approved quantity limits, base-unit label, active state, and explicit expiry configuration; migrate existing products as active and require expiry configuration before first receipt. No default tracking policy may be inferred.
- Review the request-token storage/fingerprint and concurrent replay protocol; identical input returns one original receipt and cannot create a second audit event; changed input under the token fails.
- Complete Design and code review of assignment/membership locks and row-lock order. The assignment result is approved, but its locking mechanism remains proposed under G-11/G-13.
- Reconcile TORY-13's InventoryProduct trigger lock order before adopting Product-first locking (**G-11**).
- Specify receipt audit storage from approved G-09 fields and prove audit failure rolls the receipt back. The committed receipt event remains in TORY-16; it is not deferred to TORY-20.

### Later blockers and proposed bounded deferrals — NOT APPROVED

- **G-02:** maximum delay for expiry-boundary evidence remains blocking. TORY-16 may record expiry dates and the receipt event, but a proposed bound is to decide the delay before implementing expiry quarantine transitions in the expiry/history increment (TORY-20 or its predecessor). This does not defer the approved Singapore-midnight availability rule or claim G-02 closed.
- **G-05:** damaged non-quarantined and unclassified returned physical stock remain unresolved; deactivation must remain unavailable while such units are present. The mandatory disposal reason's exact format also remains open. Resolve before implementing deactivation/disposal; no generic write-off is proposed. Do not use a fake product flag to claim deactivation is complete.
- **G-09:** keep the approved committed receipt event in TORY-16 and ensure rejected requests create no committed inventory-mutation event. A bounded proposal is to decide separate rejected-attempt security/error logging before the security/audit acceptance gate or deployment; owner acceptance is required, and G-09 remains blocking until then.
- **TORY-17:** implement and verify FEFO/FIFO allocation, equal-expiry sequence/ID ties, dated-before-undated ordering, shortages, nonnegative stock, and competing stock-out races. G-01 ordering is approved; no allocation implementation belongs in TORY-16.
- **TORY-20 and other stock work:** implement authorized balance/history views, durable movement/audit history, expiry transition evidence and any disposal/deactivation workflow at their own scope. Preserve the G-02 delay and G-05 physical-stock blockers. Other open CSV, forecast and G-11 concurrency cases remain with their dependent increments.
