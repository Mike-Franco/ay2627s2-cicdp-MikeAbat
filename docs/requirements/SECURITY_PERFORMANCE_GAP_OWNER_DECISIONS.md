## G-07 — invitation, reset and session timing

### Original blocking ambiguity and approved disposition

The original gap lacked token lifetimes, replacement/revocation rules, session clocks, throttle outcomes and recovery behaviour. The 3 November Option A approval fixes those observable policies below. BR-026 still requires deactivated Staff to lose protected access, including an already issued session; the invalidation mechanism belongs to Design. G-13 still governs catalogue and assignment authority. No Manager permission beyond approved same-business/inventory scope is implied.

### Owner-approved Option A and historical alternatives

**Every Option A value and outcome in this table is OWNER-APPROVED on 3 November 2026. Option B remains an unapproved historical alternative.** Calendar/day boundaries, response mechanisms, privacy-preserving keys and concurrent enforcement are Design details; no extra lockout, ban or exception is approved.

| Material decision | Option A — owner-approved 3 November 2026 | Option B — unapproved historical alternative | Trade-off / rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Staff invitation lifetime and resend | **24 hours** from issue; one outstanding invitation per target Staff identity; only the latest resend works; redemption is single-use. An authorised Manager may revoke a pending invitation and resend within BR-027 scope. | **72 hours**; older invitations remain valid until redemption/expiry. | A limits exposure and stale assignments but needs resend support. B reduces missed invitations but leaves more usable links. Neither authorises cross-business invitation or a Manager-set password. |
| Reset lifetime and multiple requests | **30 minutes** from issue; a new request invalidates that account's earlier unused reset links; successful reset invalidates all outstanding links and requires fresh login. | **2 hours**; multiple issued links may coexist until one succeeds, after which all become invalid. | A narrows the stolen-email-link window but may frustrate delayed mail. B is simpler with Django-style password-change invalidation, but leaves more simultaneous links. Both preserve BR-044 single use. |
| Session clocks | **30-minute idle timeout and 8-hour absolute lifetime from session creation**. Successful authenticated protected requests count as idle-timer activity; activity cannot extend the absolute limit. | **60-minute idle timeout and 24-hour absolute lifetime**. | A reduces stolen-cookie exposure but increases sign-ins. B helps long shifts but increases exposure. Clock storage/check mechanism is Design. |
| Logout and deactivation | Logout invalidates the current session immediately. Staff deactivation denies **every** existing Staff session on its next protected request, with no grace period. Successful password reset also revokes all prior sessions. | Logout still invalidates current session and deactivation still denies protected access, but other sessions after reset expire only on their existing clocks. | Deactivation denial is already required by BR-026. All-session reset revocation is an approved added security outcome; implementation may require a session generation or equivalent check. |
| Suspected stolen session | On a credible report, an owner-controlled incident response revokes all sessions for that account, requires password reset and preserves incident evidence without exposing the cookie or password. | Rely on idle/absolute expiry and advise immediate Staff deactivation where authorised. | Active revocation better limits misuse but needs an owner-operated recovery path. This row does not give Managers a new general account or session administration permission. |
| Login abuse | Independent limits: **5 failed attempts per account identity per 15 minutes** and **20 failed attempts per source IP per 15 minutes**; throttle further attempts for that window, with generic feedback. | **10 per identity and 50 per IP per 15 minutes**, with adaptive delay after the first failures. | A resists guessing with simple rules but may inconvenience shared networks; B has fewer false positives but greater attack allowance. Do not rely on an IP-plus-account pair as the only limit. No permanent account lockout is proposed. |
| Invitation/reset abuse | **3 reset requests per account identity per hour**, **20 reset requests per source IP per hour**, and **5 Staff invitations per Manager per day**; excess requests do not send mail. | **6, 40 and 10** respectively for the same windows. | A reduces inbox flooding and cost pressure; B tolerates retries but increases abuse. | Enumeration and feedback | Same public wording and materially equivalent status/timing for nonexistent, inactive and active emails on login/reset request; accepted reset request says only “If eligible, instructions will be sent.” Invitation target collisions get a scope-safe Manager response without exposing another business's account. | Keep generic public responses, but provide more specific delivery or account-state guidance only through an authenticated, in-scope Manager recovery workflow. | Both protect anonymous account existence. A is simpler; B may aid support but needs careful authorization and more test cases. Internal operational evidence may distinguish failure without exposing credentials or crossing BR-027 scope. Exact copy and timing technique are Design details. |
| Email failure and recovery | No account activation, password change or invitation redemption is claimed until the corresponding authorised action succeeds. Never email passwords or expose raw tokens in public responses/logs. | Fail closed and require an owner-operated manual recovery procedure after delivery failure. | A supports recovery without granting new account powers; B has less application workflow but may delay use. Delivery provider, retry timing and no-cost feasibility remain G-10 open. |
| MFA scope | Explicitly **outside MVP**, with a documented risk and future change control; no MFA claim in acceptance. | Add MFA for Managers now, including enrollment and recovery. | A fits the fixed solo-developer schedule but leaves password-only risk; B improves account protection while adding delivery, recovery and testing work. Owner must decide, and any MFA scope change must be recorded. |

**Owner-approved G-07 Option A — 3 November 2026:** the first option in every row is binding observable Requirements behaviour. For session idleness, **successful authenticated protected requests** constitute activity; activity never extends the **8-hour absolute lifetime from session creation**. No rate-limit window algorithm, extra lockout period, hosted email feasibility or implementation is implied. MFA is **outside MVP**; password-only exposure is a recorded risk, and adding MFA later requires change control.

### Design boundary for token and session handling

The owner-approved outcomes fix lifetime, invalidation, permission and error behaviour. Design chooses token representation and persistence, clock source, calendar/day boundaries, mail retry, session store, privacy-preserving identity/IP keys, concurrent throttling enforcement and response formatting. A suitable design should use cryptographically unpredictable purpose-bound tokens, avoid storing a reusable raw invitation token, compare securely, bind redemption to the intended identity and approved business/inventory scope, and keep tokens/passwords out of logs and referrer-leaking URLs. Django's built-in password-reset machinery is a candidate, but its defaults alone must not be assumed to implement “latest request wins,” all-session revocation or invitation semantics; Design must demonstrate the approved outcomes. HTTPS, HttpOnly and CSRF remain mandatory BR-045 controls. [Django 5.2 sessions](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/5.2/topics/http/sessions/) and [OWASP Forgot Password](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Forgot_Password_Cheat_Sheet.html) are design references, not selected implementation.

### Deterministic acceptance examples — planned, not executed

These examples specify the **approved Option A** outcome; they are not executed tests. At a stated limit, the permitted count is inclusive and the next request is rejected; exact sliding/calendar-window boundaries remain Design details.

| Case | Expected observable outcome under approved Option A |
|---|---|
| Manager issues Staff invite at 09:00; Staff opens it at 09:01 next day, or reuses it after successful setup. | Expired at 24 hours; reuse fails. Neither attempt creates a second account, changes a password or grants inventory access. |
| Manager resends an unredeemed invitation while the first is still within 24 hours; then Manager deactivates that Staff account. | Only the newest link may complete setup; deactivated Staff cannot redeem a pending link or access protected operations. Manager cannot broaden assignments beyond BR-027. |
| An authorised Manager revokes an unused invitation within its 24-hour window; the invitee follows that link. | The revoked link fails without account activation or password setup. A later authorised resend issues the sole usable link for that identity. |
| A user requests reset links R1 then R2; tries R1, uses R2, then tries R2 again. | R1 fails as superseded; R2 works once within 30 minutes; reuse fails. Existing sessions require fresh login after successful reset under approved Option A. |
| Reset is requested for an unknown or deactivated email and for an active email. | Public status/message does not disclose account existence. No reset mail grants access to an unknown or inactive account. Excess requests beyond the approved cap send no additional email. |
| User is idle for 30 minutes; separately, successful authenticated protected requests occur before each idle expiry but the session reaches 8 hours from creation. | The next protected request requires authentication at either expiry. Successful protected activity resets only the idle clock; it never extends the absolute lifetime. |
| User logs out; separately, a Staff account is deactivated while its session cookie is held by another browser. | The logged-out session cannot access protected operations. Deactivated Staff's old session is denied on the next protected request, with history attribution preserved. |
| A credible stolen-session report is received for an active account. | The owner-controlled response revokes all account sessions and requires password reset before further protected access. Existing actor/transaction history stays attributable; no Manager-wide revocation permission is inferred. |
| Authenticated user submits a stock mutation without valid CSRF proof, or with an unassigned inventory ID. | Reject without stock/audit mutation. A valid session cannot bypass CSRF or BR-022 inventory scope. |
| Five failures for one identity or 20 from one IP occur within their respective 15-minute windows; another attempt follows. | The first five/twenty count toward the approved thresholds; the sixth identity attempt or twenty-first IP attempt is throttled with generic feedback, including for an unknown identity. The two limits are independent. No extra ban period is inferred. |
| Three reset requests for one identity or 20 from one IP occur within an hour; a fourth/twenty-first follows. A Manager sends five invitations that day; a sixth follows. | The requests at the approved limits are permitted if otherwise eligible; the next request in each category is throttled and sends no email. No raw token or credential appears in public response/logs. |
| Email delivery fails for an invited Staff member or reset requester. | No password is disclosed; a safe eligible retry/resend is possible within approved scope. No account is treated as activated or reset merely because a request or send attempt occurred. |

**Remaining questions:** Design must define invitation/reset clock persistence, exact calendar/day window boundaries, concurrency-safe token and throttle enforcement, privacy-preserving identity keys, response mechanism, email retry and session invalidation. Any additional per-target invitation cap is **unapproved** and would need a separate owner decision. G-09 rejected-attempt security logging, G-10 email/HTTPS/PostgreSQL/backup feasibility and G-13 authority remain separate open gaps. **Design/Testing evidence:** clock-controlled token and session tests, concurrent threshold tests, generic responses without enumeration, cross-business scope, delivery failure, CSRF, old-session denial and compromise response, with revision/environment/results. No implementation or test is claimed.

**G-07 status: OWNER-APPROVED OPTION A; REQUIREMENTS BEHAVIOUR RESOLVED, DESIGN VERIFICATION OPEN.**

## G-08 — performance benchmark definition

### Original blocking ambiguity and approved partial disposition

NFR-006 fixes **p95 ordinary request latency ≤2 seconds** and **p95 forecast request latency ≤3 seconds**. The 3 November approval now fixes Envelope B, sample/run rules, controlled-local-network timing, separate CSV completion and repeated race correctness. **Exact ordinary and forecast/reorder endpoint mixes, a verified controlled benchmark environment, and cold-start pass/fail treatment remain acceptance-defining questions.** Warm local results cannot establish hosted cold-start or remote-network performance. Neither approved p95 limit is relaxed.
