## G-07 — invitation, reset and session timing

### Why this blocks Requirements approval

“Time-limited,” “secure” and “revoked access” cannot be tested deterministically without token lifetimes, replacement/revocation rules, session clocks, throttle outcomes and recovery behaviour. A generic success page can still disclose account existence through timing, status or email side effects. BR-026 requires deactivated Staff to lose protected access, including an already issued session; the session invalidation mechanism belongs to Design. G-13 still governs catalogue and assignment authority. No Manager permission beyond approved same-business/inventory scope is implied.

### Owner-visible policy options and trade-offs

All numbers and choices in this table are **PROPOSED, NOT APPROVED**.

| Material decision | Option A — conservative, recommended | Option B — lower-friction alternative | Trade-off / owner choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Staff invitation lifetime and resend | **24 hours** from issue; one outstanding invitation per target Staff identity; only the latest resend works; redemption is single-use. An authorised Manager may revoke a pending invitation and resend within BR-027 scope. | **72 hours**; older invitations remain valid until redemption/expiry. | A limits exposure and stale assignments but needs resend support. B reduces missed invitations but leaves more usable links. Neither authorises cross-business invitation or a Manager-set password. |
| Reset lifetime and multiple requests | **30 minutes** from issue; a new request invalidates that account's earlier unused reset links; successful reset invalidates all outstanding links and requires fresh login. | **2 hours**; multiple issued links may coexist until one succeeds, after which all become invalid. | A narrows the stolen-email-link window but may frustrate delayed mail. B is simpler with Django-style password-change invalidation, but leaves more simultaneous links. Both preserve BR-044 single use. |
| Session clocks | **30-minute idle timeout and 8-hour absolute lifetime**, measured from successful authentication; neither clock is reset by a mere page refresh unless the approved activity definition says it is. | **60-minute idle timeout and 24-hour absolute lifetime**. | A reduces stolen-cookie exposure but increases sign-ins. B helps long shifts but increases exposure. The owner must define which authorised requests count as activity; the exact storage/check mechanism is Design. |
| Logout and deactivation | Logout invalidates the current session immediately. Staff deactivation denies **every** existing Staff session on its next protected request, with no grace period. Successful password reset also revokes all prior sessions. | Logout still invalidates current session and deactivation still denies protected access, but other sessions after reset expire only on their existing clocks. | Deactivation denial is already required by BR-026. All-session reset revocation is a proposed added security outcome; implementation may require a session generation or equivalent check. |
| Suspected stolen session | On a credible report, an owner-controlled incident response revokes all sessions for that account, requires password reset and preserves incident evidence without exposing the cookie or password. | Rely on idle/absolute expiry and advise immediate Staff deactivation where authorised. | Active revocation better limits misuse but needs an owner-operated recovery path. This row does not give Managers a new general account or session administration permission. |
| Login abuse | Independent limits: **5 failed attempts per account identity per 15 minutes** and **20 failed attempts per source IP per 15 minutes**; throttle further attempts for that window, with generic feedback. | **10 per identity and 50 per IP per 15 minutes**, with adaptive delay after the first failures. | A resists guessing with simple rules but may inconvenience shared networks; B has fewer false positives but greater attack allowance. Do not rely on an IP-plus-account pair as the only limit. No permanent account lockout is proposed. |
| Invitation/reset abuse | **3 reset requests per account identity per hour**, **20 reset requests per source IP per hour**, and **5 Staff invitations per Manager per day**; excess requests do not send mail. | **6, 40 and 10** respectively for the same windows. | A reduces inbox flooding and cost pressure; B tolerates retries but increases abuse. The owner must confirm whether invited target email also needs a separate cap. |
| Enumeration and feedback | Same public wording and materially equivalent status/timing for nonexistent, inactive and active emails on login/reset request; accepted reset request says only “If eligible, instructions will be sent.” Invitation target collisions get a scope-safe Manager response without exposing another business's account. | Keep generic public responses, but provide more specific delivery or account-state guidance only through an authenticated, in-scope Manager recovery workflow. | Both protect anonymous account existence. Exact copy and timing technique are Design details. |
| Email failure and recovery | No account activation, password change or invitation redemption is claimed until the corresponding authorised action succeeds. Delivery provider, retry timing and no-cost feasibility remain G-10 open. |
| MFA scope | Explicitly **outside MVP**, with a documented risk and future change control; no MFA claim in acceptance. | Add MFA for Managers now, including enrollment and recovery. | A fits the fixed solo-developer schedule but leaves password-only risk; B improves account protection while adding delivery, recovery and testing work. Owner must decide, and any MFA scope change must be recorded. |

**Recommended G-07 package — PROPOSED, NOT APPROVED:** choose Option A in each row, subject to owner review of shared-IP false positives, email latency and the feasibility of session revocation. Security and inventory-scope correctness outweigh lower-friction token reuse for this MVP. These candidate numbers are policy choices, not Django defaults and not evidence that an email or zero-cost hosting provider works.

### Design boundary for token and session handling

The owner decides observable lifetime, invalidation, permission and error outcomes. Design chooses token representation, durable state, clock source, mail retry, session store, throttling store and response formatting. A suitable design should use cryptographically unpredictable purpose-bound tokens, avoid storing a reusable raw invitation token, compare securely, bind redemption to the intended identity and approved business/inventory scope, and keep tokens/passwords out of logs and URLs that leak through referrers. Django's built-in password-reset machinery is a candidate for reset links, but its defaults alone must not be assumed to implement “latest request wins,” all-session revocation or invitation semantics; Design must demonstrate those added outcomes if approved. HTTPS, HttpOnly and CSRF remain mandatory BR-045 controls. Rate limits must persist across concurrent requests and must not reveal whether an account exists. [Django 5.2 sessions](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/5.2/topics/http/sessions/) and [OWASP Forgot Password](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Forgot_Password_Cheat_Sheet.html) are design references, not selected implementation.

### Deterministic proposed acceptance examples

These examples apply **only if the owner approves Option A and its numbers**; they are not executed tests.

| Case | Expected observable outcome under proposed Option A |
|---|---|
| Manager issues Staff invite at 09:00; Staff opens it at 09:01 next day, or reuses it after successful setup. | Expired at 24 hours; reuse fails. Neither attempt creates a second account, changes a password or grants inventory access. |
| Manager resends an unredeemed invitation while the first is still within 24 hours; then Manager deactivates that Staff account. | Only the newest link may complete setup; deactivated Staff cannot redeem a pending link or access protected operations. Manager cannot broaden assignments beyond BR-027. |
| A user requests reset links R1 then R2; tries R1, uses R2, then tries R2 again. | R1 fails as superseded; R2 works once within 30 minutes; reuse fails. Existing sessions require fresh login after successful reset under proposed A. |
| Reset is requested for an unknown or deactivated email and for an active email. | Public status/message does not disclose account existence. No reset mail grants access to an unknown or inactive account. Excess requests beyond the proposed cap send no additional email. |
| User is idle for 30 minutes, or remains active past 8 hours from login. | Next protected request requires authentication in either case; a shorter idle period with activity before the absolute limit may continue. The activity definition is an owner choice. |
| User logs out; separately, a Staff account is deactivated while its session cookie is held by another browser. | The logged-out session cannot access protected operations. Deactivated Staff's old session is denied on the next protected request, with history attribution preserved. |
| A credible stolen-session report is received for an active account. | Under proposed Option A, the owner-controlled response revokes all account sessions and requires password reset before further protected access. Existing actor/transaction history stays attributable; no Manager-wide revocation permission is inferred. |
| Authenticated user submits a stock mutation without valid CSRF proof, or with an unassigned inventory ID. | Reject without stock/audit mutation. A valid session cannot bypass CSRF or BR-022 inventory scope. |
| Sixth failed login for one identity inside 15 minutes, or twenty-first failure from one IP inside 15 minutes. | Further attempts are throttled with generic feedback, including when the identity is nonexistent. Other legitimate accounts sharing an IP are considered in owner review. |
| Fourth reset request for an identity within an hour, or sixth Staff invitation by a Manager that day. | Excess request is throttled and sends no email; no raw token or credential appears in response/logs. |
| Email delivery fails for an invited Staff member or reset requester. | No password is disclosed; a safe eligible retry/resend is possible within approved scope. No account is treated as activated or reset merely because a request or send attempt occurred. |

**Owner decisions still needed:** approve/amend each Option A number and outcome (including exact boundary inclusivity), choose reset-link replacement policy, define session activity and owner-controlled compromise response, determine invited-email cap and MFA scope, and decide whether G-09 separately records rejected authentication attempts. Confirm email recovery policy without selecting an unverified provider. **Design/Testing dependencies:** implement and verify token/session/rate-limit clocks and concurrent submissions, generic responses and no enumeration side channel, cross-business scope, delivery failure, CSRF and old-session denial. Record revision, environment, clock control and results. G-10 email/HTTPS feasibility and G-13 authority remain separate open gaps.

## G-08 — performance benchmark definition

### Why this blocks Requirements approval

NFR-006 fixes **p95 ordinary request latency ≤2 seconds** and **p95 forecast request latency ≤3 seconds**. It does not fix a workload, dataset, concurrency, environment, cold-start rule or measurement boundary. A tiny fixture or omitted slow endpoint could pass without representing Tory. Conversely, a free-host sleep/wake delay could dominate a run without showing application behaviour. The benchmark must also check NFR-003 correctness under simultaneous stock-outs. No relaxation of either approved p95 limit is proposed.

### Candidate benchmark envelopes

Every number, size, concurrency count and hardware assumption in this table is **PROPOSED, NOT APPROVED**. “Products” and “batches” are totals across the stated businesses; history rows are committed stock/audit movements. Each fixture includes expired and quarantined units and enough eligible stock for repeatable mutations. B and C additionally include two or more businesses and different inventory scopes.

| Envelope | Proposed fixture and concurrency | Proposed sampling | Use and trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Reproducible minimum** | **1 business, 2 inventories, 100 products, 300 batches, 5,000 history rows; 2 simultaneous users / 2 in-flight requests.** | **200 measured ordinary + 200 forecast requests per run; 2 runs.** | Fast local smoke benchmark. Cannot demonstrate cross-business isolation or a representative larger history, so it should not be the sole acceptance envelope. |
| **B. Representative baseline — recommended** | **2 businesses, 4 inventories total, 500 products, 2,000 batches, 20,000 history rows; 5 simultaneous users / up to 5 in-flight requests.** | **500 measured ordinary + 500 forecast requests per run; 3 independent runs.** | Covers tenant boundaries, mixed operations and meaningful history while remaining plausible for a solo developer on local Django/PostgreSQL. Feasibility is unmeasured. |
| **C. Optional stretch** | **5 businesses, 20 inventories total, 2,000 products, 10,000 batches, 100,000 history rows; 10 simultaneous users / up to 10 in-flight requests.** | **1,000 measured ordinary + 1,000 forecast requests per run; 3 runs.** | Reveals scaling headroom but may require data generation and hardware beyond the SGD 0 MVP. It does not replace B or alter approved thresholds. |

**Recommended G-08 envelope — PROPOSED, NOT APPROVED:** B as the formal representative acceptance workload, A for repeatable development checks, C only as an optional diagnostic. Proposed execution host assumption for B: local or no-charge controlled runner with **at least 2 virtual CPUs and 4 GiB RAM**, Django application and PostgreSQL on the same host or controlled local network, fixed software versions and identical fixture seed. Record actual CPU, RAM, storage, OS, PostgreSQL/Django versions and background load. No zero-cost host is certified to meet B; G-10 remains open.

### Proposed workload and measurement contract

| Decision | Recommended proposal — not approved | Alternative and trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary request mix | In each B run: **20% authenticated inventory view, 20% product search, 20% ordinary stock-in, 20% ordinary stock-out, 20% login**. Seed enough separate stock so repeated writes remain valid; exclude rejected requests from the latency sample but test them separately. | Equal counts per endpoint rather than one aggregate; reveals a slow class but creates more pass/fail cells. Owner may require both aggregate and per-class reporting. |
| Forecast/reorder class | **50% seven-day forecast view, 50% forecast-dependent reorder view/calculation**, each with seven verified prior Singapore days and mixed zero/nonzero demand; apply the approved **≤3 s p95** to this class. | Forecast-only p95 with reorder as diagnostic; simpler but might hide a slow dependent recommendation. |
| CSV import | Measure maximum approved **1,000-data-row** valid `sku,change` import as a **separate completion-time class**; propose **≤30 seconds** from request start to complete response, plus full atomicity and audit checks. Do not silently place it in the ordinary p95 bucket. | Include CSV in ordinary p95 ≤2 s; stricter and possibly unrealistic for a bounded bulk operation. Owner must explicitly decide classification and any separate metric; NFR-006's two approved limits are unchanged. |
| Timing boundary | Controlled **HTTP client request start through complete response body**, including Django, PostgreSQL and local network/loopback; exclude fixture setup and client think time. Record server-side timings separately for diagnosis. | Server-side only: more reproducible across networks but less user-facing. Remote WAN-inclusive: user-facing but variable with location/provider. Owner chooses the acceptance boundary. |
| Warm-up and p95 | Before each B run, **20 warm-up requests per endpoint class**; then collect the specified samples. Sort successful measured durations and take the **nearest-rank p95: element `ceil(0.95 × n)`** (1-indexed). Require **all 3 B runs** to meet each approved p95 limit; report count, p50, p95, max and failures. | One pooled run or average of run p95s is cheaper but can conceal an unstable run. A cold-start series can be reported separately; inclusion in the formal gate is an owner choice. |
| Repeatability and environment | Fix fixture generator seed, timezone, request mix, software revision, database state and concurrency schedule; reset stock between runs. Proposed B hardware assumption above. Record client/server topology and network conditions. | Benchmark only on a candidate free host; closer to demo conditions but may be non-repeatable or unavailable. Hosted staging remains a separate G-10 feasibility check. |
| Correctness under load | At least **20 simultaneous two-request races** for one last eligible unit under NFR-003; exactly one issue commits in each race, no negative balance/double allocation/partial audit. Also verify authorised business/inventory scope and successful CSV all-or-nothing under load. | A few manual races are cheaper but cannot supply reliable repeatability evidence. The transaction mechanism remains G-11 Design work. |
| Failure disposition | If B exceeds either approved p95 target, record a **fail** with raw timings, environment and revision; diagnose, optimise and retest. If the SGD 0 environment cannot run or meet it, record the feasibility blocker and seek an explicit owner schedule/scope/budget decision. | Quietly lower the fixture, omit slow classes or relax thresholds: unacceptable without a new owner approval and change record. |

The proposed request mix does not define every Tory screen. Search, login, stock-in, stock-out, forecast, reorder and CSV are all represented or separately measured. A failed or invalid request must still satisfy authorization/atomicity; latency success cannot compensate for incorrect stock, missing audit or cross-business access. “Five simultaneous users” is a **candidate workload assumption**, not a measured production peak. Neither local benchmark success nor a PoC stock-out test proves hosted p95 performance.

### Deterministic proposed acceptance examples

These apply **only if the owner selects B and its measurement contract**; no test has run.

| Case | Expected evidence/outcome under proposed B |
|---|---|
| Three B runs produce ordinary p95 `1.7`, `1.9`, `2.1` seconds and forecast/reorder p95 below 3 seconds. | **Fail ordinary NFR-006** because the third run exceeds the approved 2-second target; do not average p95s. |
| One B run has 500 ordinary samples; the 475th sorted duration is 1.98 s and the 476th is 2.40 s. | Nearest-rank p95 is the 475th value, **1.98 s**, for that run; retain raw durations and failures for review. |
| Five clients submit mixed valid ordinary and forecast/reorder requests against the stated fixture. | Measure each class under its own approved p95 threshold; report endpoint mix and failures. A 1,000-row CSV result is reported separately under the proposed 30-second completion metric. |
| Two authorised stock-out requests compete for the same final eligible unit, repeated 20 times. | In every race exactly one allocation commits; the other does not oversell or leave a partial stock/audit result. Any correctness failure fails acceptance regardless of p95. |
| Candidate free staging host sleeps before first request and warm B requests pass. | Report cold-start timing separately and do not claim hosted user-facing performance from the controlled warm run. Owner decides whether cold starts join the formal gate; G-10 provider feasibility remains open. |
| Controlled B host cannot meet approved p95 or no SGD 0 service can sustain the fixture. | Record failure/feasibility evidence and escalate for owner change control. Do not reduce the fixture or relax 2/3-second limits without approval. |

**Owner decisions still needed:** choose A/B/C or amend fixture and concurrency, request grouping and CSV classification/completion bound, client/network/cold-start boundary, warm-up/sample/pass rule and controlled environment specification. Decide whether aggregate class p95 alone suffices or each endpoint also needs a bound. Accept G-10 feasibility risk explicitly if staging differs from the acceptance host. **Design/Testing dependencies:** specify reproducible generator, load tool, endpoint scripts, authenticated client setup, observation-coverage fixture, reset procedure and raw-timing capture; run PostgreSQL-backed performance and two-session atomicity tests only after Design/Implementation gates. Evidence must include revision, host, software versions, seed, sample counts, sorted durations/p95 calculation, error counts, transaction outcomes and repeat runs. No provider, test result or measured capacity is claimed.
