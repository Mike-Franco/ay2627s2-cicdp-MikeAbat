## 1. Introduction & Motivation

Tory is a browser-based inventory and demand forecasting system for small businesses with multiple stock locations. Scattered records make it difficult to reconcile stock, explain changes, and tell whether physical stock can still be issued. A typical workflow is for assigned Staff to receive and issue stock, and for a Manager to review the movement history, reconcile counts, and use demand and reorder information to plan replenishment. Traceable records and more informed replenishment are intended benefits. Effects on real business outcomes remain unmeasured. [Project charter](../planning/PROJECT_CHARTER.md), [SRS](../requirements/SOFTWARE_REQUIREMENTS_SPECIFICATION.md).

Django 5.2 LTS with PostgreSQL was approved during Planning on 29 October 2026. The evaluation held PostgreSQL constant and scored Django 4.10/5, FastAPI 3.60/5 and Express 3.55/5. These are weighted design judgments. Django brought an integrated ORM, migrations and authentication to a data-heavy project, while the PostgreSQL proof of concept supplied bounded evidence for transactions, constraints, CSV rollback and a competing stock-out. The PoC was a separate feasibility exercise. The alternatives were assessed without comparative implementations. Django Templates and Bootstrap were selected separately on 3 November for server-rendered forms and pages with targeted JavaScript, reducing the integration work of a separate client application. [Backend decision](../decisions/ADR-001-backend-and-database.md), [PoC evidence](../POC_RESULTS.md), [frontend decision](../decisions/ADR-003-frontend-architecture.md).

## 2. Core Architecture & Design Principles

Tory uses a modular Django application with server-rendered views and a shared PostgreSQL database. Middleware supplies authentication, sessions and CSRF protection. Named routes send requests to views, which resolve the user's scope, bind forms, call services and render templates or redirect. The application boundaries follow responsibilities: `accounts` manages authentication and account workflows, `inventory_access` models business membership and assignment, `catalogue` owns products and reorder settings, and `receiving` owns stock movements, audit evidence, imports and demand coverage logic. The catalogue forecast view handles coverage requests and calls receiving services. PostgreSQL stores the relational state and enforces transaction and trigger constraints. [Architecture and stock-out sequence](../design/ARCHITECTURE.md), [routes](../../config/urls.py), [settings](../../config/settings.py), [catalogue views](../../catalogue/views.py).

| Native Django feature | Use in Tory |
|---|---|
| ORM models and migrations | `InventoryProduct` links a catalogue product to an inventory, with database-backed constraints and versioned schema changes. Django provides the model and migration mechanisms. Same-business relationship checks and movement invariants include custom PostgreSQL SQL installed through migrations. [Model](../../catalogue/models.py#L62), [migration](../../catalogue/migrations/0001_initial.py), [assignment trigger](../../inventory_access/migrations/0002_enforce_assignment_business_match.py). |
| Authentication and database sessions | Tory uses a custom email-based `AbstractUser` model, Django's authentication helpers and database session backend. Tory's `protected_view` applies idle and absolute session limits, and custom queries enforce active business membership and inventory assignment. [User model](../../accounts/models.py#L36), [login view](../../accounts/views.py#L28), [session wrapper](../../accounts/sessions.py#L24), [scope queries](../../inventory_access/authorization.py#L7). |
| Forms, CSRF and templates | `ManualStockOutForm` validates whole-unit input. The issue view binds the POST body and uses `cleaned_data`. The template posts a CSRF token checked by middleware. The service separately verifies whether the actor can issue from that inventory and whether stock is eligible. [Form](../../receiving/forms.py#L40), [view](../../receiving/views.py#L142), [template](../../templates/receiving/issue_stock.html), [middleware](../../config/settings.py#L62). |
| Atomic transactions and locking | `issue_stock` uses `transaction.atomic()` and `select_for_update()`. Django supplies the transaction and ORM interfaces. Tory chooses the lock order and allocation policy, and PostgreSQL supplies row locks and enforces write and deferred expiry checks. [Stock service](../../receiving/services.py#L325), [expiry guards](../../receiving/migrations/0011_expiry_quarantine_guards.py). |

The manual issue sequence illustrates why these layers matter. The view checks the session and assigned inventory. The service rechecks scope under locks, locks the product, inventory, association and eligible batches, then allocates dated batches by first-expiry-first-out (FEFO), followed by undated batches in receipt order. The stock-out, allocations, reductions in batch and physical balances, and demand audit commit together. A rejected service request or a failed database write or commit leaves no partial movement. After success the service returns a `StockOut` instance, and the view uses its primary key for the confirmation redirect. [Stock-out sequence](../design/ARCHITECTURE.md#manual-stock-out), [service](../../receiving/services.py#L325), [regression tests](../../tests/test_stock_out.py).

The ERD groups business membership and product scope, account workflows, receipt and issue evidence, adjustments, expiry and disposal, lifecycle, CSV import and demand coverage. The forecast and reorder recommendation are computed outputs. Stored issues, eligible CSV attribution and Manager-verified coverage supply the previous seven complete Singapore days. A valid forecast feeds a reorder point calculated as average daily demand times lead time plus safety stock. The alert compares that point with expiry-aware available stock. Missing coverage or settings can make a result unavailable, keeping missing evidence distinct from zero demand. [ERD and derived outputs](../design/ERD.md), [demand logic](../../receiving/demand.py#L122), [reorder logic](../../catalogue/reorder.py#L32).

## 4. Critical Analysis & Conclusion

Django was a reasonable fit for an inventory system with structured forms, authenticated roles and relational records. A shared framework for validation, templates and migrations made the path from request to database explicit. Server rendering limits the frontend integration burden for a solo project and supports the Products page's GET form. The tradeoff is less scope for rich client interaction without extra JavaScript or separate components. The use of PostgreSQL constraints and triggers gives important invariants a second enforcement point, while coupling the application to a particular database and requiring integration tests on that database. Locks avoid competing allocations but create contention and demand consistent acquisition order. A simple moving average is explainable, but relies on verified observations and cannot account for longer seasonal patterns within this design. [Architecture](../design/ARCHITECTURE.md), [frontend decision](../decisions/ADR-003-frontend-architecture.md), [demand contract](../decisions/TORY-21-csv-demand-contract.md).

**Performance remains open.** TORY-26 recorded ordinary HTTP p95 values of **10.774 s, 9.217 s and 8.106 s** against a **2 s** limit. All three failed. The combined forecast/reorder route measured 0.590 s, 0.592 s and 0.527 s against the 3 s target while rendering its unavailable state. The numeric path remains unverified by those runs. The separate 1,000-row CSV import took 5.419 s locally against a 30 s limit. This run used a development server and local PostgreSQL, with background load uncontrolled. Login dominated the ordinary mix, and diagnostic investigation identified shared-IP lock waiting as one contributor. These observations support further controlled measurement. They establish neither a general Django throughput limit nor hosted performance. [TORY-26 benchmark and diagnostic record](../decisions/TORY-26-integrated-validation.md), [raw timing evidence](../../benchmark-results/tory26-local.json).

**Deployment and recovery remain open.** TORY-27 added guarded local backup/restore tooling. An earlier restore comparison failed. A fresh isolated rehearsal passed data/schema comparison under the narrowly defined `captured-array-casts-v1` rule. The raw constraint hashes were unequal, and the restored target remained quarantined. A separate age rehearsal verified a local encrypt/decrypt round trip and rejected the tested wrong-key and corrupted-ciphertext cases. Daily scheduling, off-site storage and restore, durable key recovery, hosted HTTPS/email, expiry scheduler evidence, and safe application resumption remain outstanding. The proposed destination, retention, RPO and RTO remain draft policy. [Backup/restore runbook](../decisions/TORY-27-local-backup-restore.md), [local encryption record](../decisions/TORY-27-local-encryption-rehearsal.md), [draft policy](../decisions/TORY-27-offsite-backup-policy.md), [staging feasibility record](../requirements/DEPLOYMENT_FEASIBILITY_GAP_OWNER_DECISIONS.md).

On this evidence, Django with PostgreSQL is a good candidate for a small team building a form-driven system where relational integrity and auditability matter. An API-only product with separately owned clients or a real-time workload with stringent latency constraints would justify a fresh evaluation of the stack. For Tory, the priority is representative performance evidence and a staged recovery exercise before making stronger reliability or scaling claims. The project demonstrates the specified framework patterns and implemented workflows. Full SRS approval, production readiness and outstanding acceptance remain open.
